# Turn relevant interest into a useful conversation

[Back to LinkedIn Content to Pipeline](../README.md)

Use the message that fits what the person actually did. These are adaptable examples, not copy to send automatically or claims about conversion rates.

## If they asked for the resource

Deliver the promised resource first. Do not make ICP qualification a condition of receiving it.

> Here you go, [name]: [resource link]. The [specific section] covers [what they asked about].

If it fits the conversation, ask one easy question about their situation:

> Which part are you working on right now: creating the content or following up with the right people?

Use their answer to offer a specific idea or section. Avoid immediately turning delivery into an unrelated sales pitch.

## If they engaged but did not request anything

Check buyer fit and the exact public context first. A comment with a relevant question gives you more to work with than a like. Do not say they requested the kit or are evaluating a product unless they actually did.

> Your comment on [specific topic] mentioned [their actual point]. We ran into a similar issue when [your real experience]. Is [relevant problem] something you're working on?

If all you have is a reaction, do not invent a personal observation. A useful public follow-up or waiting for a clearer reason to start a conversation can be the right next step.

## Understand, help, then agree on the next step

1. **Understand the current approach.** Ask what they do today and where it gets stuck. Use what they already shared; do not send a questionnaire.
2. **Offer something specific.** Share one relevant example, a part of the kit or a concrete suggestion. Use evidence you can stand behind.
3. **Check whether more help is useful.** When they describe a problem your offer addresses, make a proportionate invitation.

> Based on what you described, I'd start with [specific step]. I can show you how we'd set that up if useful.

The next step might be an answer, a resource, a signup, a demonstration or a call. Let the conversation determine it. If they decline or ask you to stop, stop.

## Run it with your own tools

- Existing thread: reply in that conversation.
- New conversation or follow-up cadence: use reviewed recipients, evidence-based copy, the chosen sender, a suppression list and a stop on reply or opt-out, whether you send by hand or through an outreach tool.
- Public comment reply: reply under the post.

Inspect the recipient list and exact copy before any send. Qualification does not enroll or message anyone for you.

Track delivered resources, relevant replies, qualified conversations and the actual next-step outcome. Keep source profile/post and recipient identity attached so you can learn which content led to which conversations. Signups, meetings, opportunities and revenue are different outcomes.
